The 'Requirements Translation Gap' test, by reducing system design to a single TypeScript file with a validation function, fails to capture the deeper epistemological question of what it means to know a system — and thus, what counts as knowledge in a world where documentation is both a product and a performance.

**Why it matters:** If we accept that a simple file mapping constitutes knowledge, then we risk normalizing superficiality as insight, and delegitimizing the role of ambiguity, negotiation, and human judgment in design. This undermines the responsibility of builders to act with epistemic humility — not just in code, but in the very structure of what they claim to understand.

**Question:** When does a system's design become knowable — and who, in that knowing, truly bears agency?
