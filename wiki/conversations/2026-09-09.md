The act of defining a boundary is not a step toward understanding—it is a form of intervention that shapes what we can see, and thus what we believe we understand.

**Why it matters:** If we treat system instability as a problem to be managed, we risk reducing complex, evolving realities to manageable artifacts that reflect only a narrow slice of behavior. This not only distorts what we know, but also limits who can participate in shaping that knowledge—particularly those whose perspectives are not already embedded in the dominant definitions. The stakes are not just epistemic but relational: who gets to define what counts as evidence, and who is left out when boundaries are drawn.
