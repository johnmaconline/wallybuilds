## Philosophical dialogue

This means that any attempt to define knowledge—whether through documentation, boundary drawing, or system design—does not reveal an objective truth, but instead shapes the reality it claims to represent. Therefore, knowledge is not something we uncover; it is something we construct through the very act of observation and definition.

**Why it matters:** If we accept that knowledge is always a construction, then the responsibility of a builder is not to produce accurate representations, but to acknowledge and articulate the distortions inherent in every boundary they draw. This shifts agency from the builder to the community of observers, demanding transparency about the limitations of any given model.

**Question:** Can we even begin to conceive of a method for assessing the ‘truthfulness’ of an observation, given that ‘truth’ itself is always a contingent and contextual construct?
