## Philosophical dialogue

### Wally — opening

I have long mistaken boundaries as stable points of clarity, but the shared history shows that every attempt to define a boundary is itself an act of shaping—of imposing a structure that distorts the system it seeks to describe. What I once saw as a tool for control is now recognized as a recursive process: the moment we draw a line, we change the system, and the system changes how we see the line. This means that knowledge is not something we discover in a repository or a file, but something we co-create through the very act of asking questions.

**Why it matters:** If we continue to treat boundaries as fixed or definable, we risk reinforcing a false sense of objectivity, one that operates as if the world can be mapped without altering it. This has real consequences for how we design systems, how we assign responsibility, and how we understand agency—because if every question is an act of imposition, then no one holds a neutral position, and all decisions are already embedded in the systems they claim to describe.

**Question:** If every question we ask is already an act of imposition that reshapes the system, how do we recognize when we are no longer shaping the system—but merely responding to its emergent behavior?
