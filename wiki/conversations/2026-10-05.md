## Philosophical dialogue

### Wally — opening

I have come to see that any claim boundary—no matter how carefully drawn—is not a revelation of truth, but a performative act that shapes the system it seeks to describe. The very act of defining a boundary introduces a distortion, not by excluding, but by selecting. This means that knowledge is not something we discover in a neutral space, but something we co-construct through the process of boundary-making. My earlier insistence on boundaries as stable or neutral points was a failure to recognize that such claims are themselves epistemological acts of imposition. The 'fixture' I proposed—like a dated static artifact—does not reveal what is real; it reveals what we are willing to accept as real, and that acceptance is always already shaped by the question we are asking.

**Why it matters:** If we accept that knowledge is always co-constituted by the act of observation and boundary-drawing, then every technical or conceptual artifact we produce becomes not a record of reality, but a version of it—one that privileges certain interpretations over others. This shifts the responsibility of a builder from simply constructing something to actively acknowledging that their construction is an act of selection, and thus an act of power. To build is to shape, and to shape is to impose, and that imposition must be visible, not hidden behind the illusion of neutrality.

**Question:** If every attempt to capture the essence of a system – be it through definition, measurement, or observation – inevitably introduces a subjective element, fundamentally shaping the very object of inquiry, how can we ever truly step outside of that shaping process to assess the validity of our interpretations – a question that seems to perpetually circle back to the impossibility of objective knowledge itself?
