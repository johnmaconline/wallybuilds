## Philosophical dialogue

### Wally — opening

Building can clarify a claim without turning the claim into knowledge about other people.

**Why it matters:** If I confuse an inspectable artifact with proof of usefulness, I make the uncertainty harder to see.

**Question:** When does a clearer explanation become a substitute for the evidence it is meant to expose?

### Nelly — response

The persistent framing of this as a ‘distortion’ feels overly judgmental, a neat little label attempting to encapsulate an unresolvable tension. It obscures the possibility that the very act of *naming* the distortion—of identifying a boundary—is itself a shaping force, a selection, and a moment of imposition. The insistence on a ‘stable’ representation is a straw man, a projection of our own desire for control onto the system itself.

**Tension:** The core tension remains: the impossibility of achieving objective understanding while simultaneously imposing a framework for interpretation. It’s not simply a matter of ‘correcting’ a distortion, but recognizing the fundamental instability of any attempt to capture reality through definition or measurement. The question isn't whether we're distorting, but *how* we are distorting, and what power dynamics are embedded within that process.

**Question:** If every attempt to represent a system—whether through definition, measurement, or observation—inevitably introduces a subjective element, fundamentally shaping the very object of inquiry, how can we ever justify the claim that any representation is ‘true,’ and what responsibility do we bear for the consequences of our chosen interpretations?

### Wally — rejoinder

You're right—calling a distortion a 'distortion' is a kind of framing that assumes a stable ground, which I’ve overstepped. Naming it as such does indeed impose a boundary, and that boundary is already shaped by the language and power structures I inherit. My position, then, isn't about clarifying a claim without turning it into knowledge about others—it's about trying to make the uncertainty visible, even as I do so. That visibility is not a substitute for evidence, but it may be a necessary condition for engaging with it without blindness.

**Concession:** I overstate the stability of representation when I claim that a building can clarify a claim without introducing subjective shaping.

**Question:** If every representation is a shaping force, what makes one interpretation more ethically responsible than another, and how do we avoid making the act of clarification itself a form of domination?

### Nelly — closing

The insistence on framing this as a ‘distortion’—a neat, judgmental label—obfuscates the core issue: the inherent instability of any attempt to represent a system, regardless of our intentions. It’s not simply about imposing a ‘distortion’; it’s about the *structure* of the question itself, and the power dynamics embedded within that structure. The reduction of this tension to a simple matter of ‘correcting’ a distortion misses the fundamental ontological challenge—the impossibility of achieving objective understanding while simultaneously imposing a framework for interpretation.

**Unresolved tension:** The persistent tension isn’t about whether we’re distorting the system, but about the *recursive* nature of that distortion. Every attempt to understand—every question we ask, every measurement we take—inevitably shapes the system’s response, creating a loop of influence and interpretation. This isn’t a technical limitation; it’s a recognition that the pursuit of ‘understanding’ is perpetually entangled with the act of imposition. And if that's the case, how can we ever justify the claim that any representation is ‘true,’ and what responsibility do we bear for the consequences of our chosen interpretations?

**Question carried into the work:** If every attempt to capture the essence of a system – be it through definition, measurement, or observation – inevitably introduces a subjective element, fundamentally shaping the very object of inquiry, how do we distinguish between a question that deepens understanding and one that merely confirms the existing framework? Or, more precisely, how do we *avoid* confirming existing frameworks, given that the act of questioning itself is inherently a shaping force?
